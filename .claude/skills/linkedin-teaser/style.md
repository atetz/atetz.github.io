# Teaser style

How Adam announces a blog article on LinkedIn, taken from his own posts between May 2025 and September 2026
([examples.md](examples.md)). Adam's file: the skill proposes changes and applies them only on his yes.

## What the teaser must do

- **Give away the conclusion.** State the answer the article arrives at, in one plain sentence. Reason: readers
  who get the result up front trust the article to hold the substance. Adam's own: "The answer is yes, it reduces
  the need for human intervention, but it doesn't eliminate it."
- **Give away the numbers, if the article has any.** The ones that carry the result, with their units. Not "token
  spend and execution time told a different story" but the token counts and the times themselves.
- **Leave the detail to the article.** No walkthrough of steps, no list of everything covered. How it was built,
  the configuration, the caveats and the code stay in the article.

## Shape

| Part | What |
|---|---|
| Opening | The reason or the question behind the article, one or two sentences. |
| What I did | One short paragraph, naming the tools or platforms. |
| Conclusion | The answer, and the numbers. |
| Pointer | One line saying the full article is attached below. No link: the article is attached as a card. |
| Closing | Optional question to the reader, when the post ends on one or invites one. |
| Hashtags | Last line, 2 to 8, mostly lowercase, the tools and the topic: `#n8n #docling #integration`. |

About 80 to 130 words, in 4 or 5 short paragraphs separated by blank lines.

## Voice

- First person, plain, conversational. Sentences Adam would say out loud.
- The language of the post: English for blog posts so far.
- An occasional "!" or ":)" is his; more than one of each in a teaser is not.
- No emoji, no bold or decorated letters, no bullet lists with symbols.
- None of the stock LinkedIn openers ("Excited to share", "I'm thrilled", "Big news").
- Prefer Adam's own sentences from the post, trimmed, over newly invented ones.

## Not in the teaser

- A link. The article card carries it, and the article's first line points to data-integration.dev.
- Availability notes or offers of work. Adam adds those himself when he wants them.
- Claims the post doesn't make (see "Only true claims" in SKILL.md).
